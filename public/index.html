    Sounds like you?
    
                  Does it read like a person wrote it?
                  
    
                  Recruiters see a lot of AI-drafted letters, and they all sound alike. This finds the phrases and rhythms that make writing sound generated, suggests plainer wording, and points to sentences that need a detail only you could add.
                  
  
                  
  
                  This is not an AI detector. No tool can prove who wrote something. Even OpenAI withdrew its own AI-text classifier for low accuracy, and detectors regularly flag human writing. Use this to sound specific and natural, not to judge anyone.

    Resume templates
    
                        Pick the layout that fits where you are.
                        
    
                        Each template is plain, single-column and ATS-friendly. Copy the text into Word or Google Docs, replace everything in [brackets], then run it through the grader.
                        
  
                        
  
                        
  
                        
    
                        Which format should I use?
                        
    
                        
      
                        Reverse-chronological
                        Most recent job first. The default for anyone with steady work history in the same field. Recruiters expect it.
                        
      
                        Entry-level / student
                        Education, projects and activities go first because they are your strongest evidence. Keep it to one page.
                        
      
                        Combination (hybrid)
                        Skills with proof up top, a short work history below. Good for career changers or returning to work after a gap.
                        
      
                        Technical
                        Skills and projects are scanned first. Link to GitHub or a portfolio and name the tools you used in each bullet.
                        
    
                        
  
                        

                        



                        
  
                        
    Cover letter templates
    
                        One page, three ideas, one ask.
                        
    
                        A good cover letter says why this company, gives one or two proof stories the resume can't tell, and asks for the conversation. Aim for 250 to 400 words.
                        
  
                        
  
                        
    
                        The shape of a strong letter
                        Use this outline for any of the templates below.
                        
    Greeting      Dear [Hiring manager's name],
Paragraph 1   The role, why this company specifically, and your headline strength.
Paragraph 2   One proof story: situation → what you did → the measurable result.
Paragraph 3   A second proof point tied to a requirement in the posting.
Paragraph 4   What you'd bring in the first months, plus a clear ask for a conversation.
Sign-off      Sincerely, [Full name] · [phone] · [email]

                                Formatting by the numbers
                                
  
                                

  
                                
    
                                Write bullets that prove something
                                Every bullet should answer "so what?" Use either formula.
                                
    Action verb + what you did + result (with a number)
→ Cut month-end close from 8 days to 5 by automating three reconciliation reports in Excel.
    Accomplished [X], as measured by [Y], by doing [Z]
→ Raised email open rates 27% (Y) across 40,000 subscribers (X) by A/B testing subject lines weekly (Z).
    
                                No exact number? Estimate honestly ("about 30 calls a day"), use scale ("for a 12-person team"), frequency ("weekly"), or before/after ("from paper forms to a shared tracker").
                                
  
                                

  
                                
    
                                Beat the applicant tracking system
                                Most larger employers use an applicant tracking system to store, parse and search applications. Recruiters say outright auto-rejection is less common than the myths claim, but a resume the software can't read, or that doesn't use the job's keywords, is harder to find.

                                Swap "responsible for" and "helped with" for one of these. Vary them so no verb starts more than two bullets.
